# Agentry sales stories: one per agent

What to tell a client, agent by agent: the problem they live with today, what changes the day the agent goes live,
every feature in plain words, what they'll see each month, how they stay in control, and how to price it.

**How to use this:** pick the 1–3 agents that match the client's biggest leak (the "Which story for whom" table at the
end), tell the story using *their* numbers, run the "What is it worth?" sum on the call, then show the live demo
(HQ → Demo Studio, built from their own website in two minutes).

> **Honesty rules for every pitch.** Every feature below is built and running. Where something still needs an outside
> approval or provider, the agent has a **"Before you promise"** box. Read it before you sell that agent, and never
> sell around it. Example results ("₹90,000 a month") are *worked examples to run with the client's own numbers*, not
> guarantees. Prices are starting points from `docs/PRICING.md`.

**Three promises that run through every story** (use them in every pitch):

1. **Nothing goes out without your OK until you say so.** Every agent starts in *approval mode*: it prepares the
   reply, reminder or post, and a person taps Approve. When the team trusts it, they switch it to automatic, one agent at
   a time (and some, like inbox replies, only for categories where the team approved 10 drafts unchanged).
2. **It only says what's true for your business.** Answers come from the client's own knowledge, prices, invoices and
   calendar. When it doesn't know, it says "I'll have the team confirm" instead of guessing. Quotes in posts are checked
   word for word; numbers that aren't in the source are thrown out.
3. **You see everything.** Every message, decision and run is logged (Conversations, Runs, Audit log), every result is
   counted on the monthly report with its proof, and the owner gets a morning brief on WhatsApp, Telegram or email.

## 1. Inbox triage: "Your inbox, sorted before you've had your chai"

**For:** any business that lives in Gmail or Outlook: agencies, clinics, CA firms, consultants, exporters.
**The entry product.** Cheap, live in a day, and it finds the next sale for you.

### The story

Pooja runs a content agency. Every morning starts with 140 unread emails: client feedback, three price enquiries, a
supplier's newsletter, an "URGENT: wrong post went live", and a payment confirmation buried in the middle. The urgent
one gets seen at 11:40. One enquiry gets answered two days late; the prospect had already signed with someone else.

**With Agentry,** by the time Pooja opens her laptop every email is labelled in her own Gmail (*Client, Lead,
Invoice/Payment, Urgent, Promotions*), the urgent one has already pinged her and her manager on WhatsApp, and there's a
ready-to-send reply waiting under every email that needs one, written in her tone and quoting her real prices. Her
front desk just reads, tweaks if needed, and taps Send.

### Everything it does

| Feature | What the client gets |
|---|---|
| **Sorting in your own inbox** | Every new email labelled in Gmail/Outlook within a minute (seconds with instant Gmail). Up to 10 categories of their own on top (*Supplier, Job applicant…*) |
| **Ready replies** | A draft for each email that needs an answer, from the business's knowledge, prices and saved replies. If a fact is missing, the draft marks a `[gap]` instead of inventing it |
| **Writes like you** | Learns the writing style from up to 50 of their sent emails when Gmail connects |
| **Any language** | Replies in the sender's language and script: Hindi, Marathi, Tamil… Hinglish stays Hinglish |
| **Urgent alerts that escalate** | Urgent emails ping the right people on WhatsApp, Telegram or email; still unanswered after 30 minutes? The owner gets a second alert. Quiet hours respected: a 10:30 pm email alerts at 8:00 am |
| **Fraud warnings** | Changed bank details, lookalike domains, fake display names, gift-card or OTP requests get a red warning. A suspicious payment email is always urgent, and the draft says "we'll confirm by phone" |
| **Learns from corrections** | Re-label an email once and it remembers that sender; twice for a domain and it remembers the domain |
| **Team workflow** | Assign to a colleague, snooze (1 h, tomorrow 9 am, Monday), a "Mine" tab, saved replies for common answers |
| **Follow-ups** | A client or lead gone quiet for 3 days after your last email gets a follow-up draft at 10:30 am |
| **Reads attachments** | PDFs, Word, CSV: invoice numbers, amounts and dates feed the sorting and the reply |
| **Several inboxes** | sales@, accounts@, the owner's own; replies go out from the inbox the email came to; "accounts@ cards go to Finance" |
| **Daily digest + Monday summary** | Urgent still open, clients waiting longest, new leads, payments mentioned; last week in numbers |
| **Auto-send, earned** | For categories they choose, once the team has sent 10 drafts unchanged. Never urgent, suspicious or `[gap]` emails |
| **Privacy** | Email text deleted after 30 days (their choice); the summary and labels stay |

### What they'll see every month

Emails sorted, **hours saved** (about 2 minutes per email), urgent emails caught, typical first-reply time *vs before
Agentry* (measured from their own Gmail at connection), drafts sent as written / edited / discarded, fraud caught.
Plus the **Opportunity report**: "23 enquiries last month, 7 answered after 24 h, 4 never; ₹2.4 L mentioned as overdue"
→ the pitch for Invoice follow-up and the lead agents.

### Price it

Setup ₹10–15k · retainer ₹4,999–9,999/month (by email volume). Anchor: hours saved × staff cost, plus leads no longer missed.

> **Before you promise:** while Agentry's Google app is in Testing mode, each client's Gmail must be added as a test
> user and signs in again every 7 days (docs/flows/02-client-onboarding.md). Outlook has no such limit.

## 5. Invoice follow-up: "Get paid without the awkward phone calls"

**For:** agencies, B2B suppliers, CA firms, consultants, distributors: anyone with money stuck in unpaid invoices.

### The story

Rakesh's agency has ₹18 lakh outstanding across 23 invoices. Chasing is nobody's job: the accountant sends an email

**With Agentry,** Rakesh uploads his Tally/QuickBooks/Sheets export once. Every unpaid invoice gets a polite, firm,
scheduled series of reminders on email and WhatsApp, with the real amount, the invoice number and a **Razorpay or
Stripe payment link** for the exact balance. When the client replies "will pay Friday", Agentry understands it and waits
until Friday. When they pay, reminders stop that minute. When they dispute it, a person gets a card. Rakesh doesn't
make a single awkward call.

### Everything it does

| Feature | What the client gets |
|---|---|
| **Import from anything** | CSV or Excel from Tally, QuickBooks, Zoho, Sheets; columns recognised automatically |
| **Reminder schedule** | Before due, on due, then increasingly firm; 10:00 am in the client's timezone; later reminders continue the same email thread |
| **Pay-now links** | Razorpay (INR) or Stripe link for the current balance; part payment → a new link for what's left |
| **Paid = stop** | Payment webhooks mark it paid and withdraw any reminder waiting for approval |
| **Understands replies** | "Paid, UTR…", "will pay on the 15th" (waits), "send the invoice again", "we already paid", disputes, part payments: each goes to the right next step or to a person |
| **Resolve in one click** | Dispute resolved: reopen · Write off · Void · Confirm paid · Match a payment to an invoice |
| **When reminders run out** | "Still unpaid: time for a call" card with a Call button |
| **Clear opt-out** | Only a real "STOP" / "unsubscribe" stops reminders, not "stop by tomorrow" |
| **Finance-only** | Front desk never sees money; finance, owner and manager do |

### What they'll see

Money recovered (each with the reminders and payment as proof), what's still overdue, and every customer reply worked to a decision.

## 11. Bookkeeping assistant: "Snap the bill. It's in Tally."

**For:** small businesses drowning in paper bills, and **CA firms** with many clients.

### The story

Every month-end, a restaurant owner hands a carry bag of crumpled bills to his accountant. Half are faded, some are

check digit valid, is it a duplicate? Clean bills are filed at once ("Got it: FreshMart, ₹1,254.76 on 28 Oct, filed
under Groceries"). Doubtful ones go to a person with the doubtful field highlighted next to the photo. At month-end:
**Export to Tally** in one click, every voucher balanced.

### Everything it does

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Any way in | Portal upload (many at once), WhatsApp, Telegram, Gmail attachments |
| Reads everything | Vendor, GSTIN, number, date, line items, taxes, total, category, payment method |
| Checks with code | Lines add up, subtotal + taxes = total, CGST = SGST, GSTIN check digit, date sanity, duplicates |
| Honest confidence | A smudged figure is never "98% sure": guessed fields are capped and sent to a person |
| Review screen | The photo beside the fields, doubtful ones highlighted; accept, fix or reject |
| Budgets | Per category per month; an alert at 80% |
| Monthly summary | On the 1st: spend by category vs last month, top vendors, what's still to check |
| Export | Tally XML purchase vouchers (balanced), CSV, or straight into **Google Sheets** |
| CA firm view | One screen across all their clients: to check, filed, to export, the month's total |
